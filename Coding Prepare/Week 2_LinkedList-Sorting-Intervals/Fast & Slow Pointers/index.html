🔄 Fast & Slow Pointers

Two-Pointer Algorithm Pattern

What is the Fast & Slow Pointer Pattern?

The Fast and Slow Pointer technique (also known as Floyd's Tortoise and Hare algorithm) uses two pointers that move through a data structure at different speeds. This pattern is particularly useful for solving problems involving cycles and finding middle elements.

Key Applications:

  • Detecting cycles in linked lists
  • Finding the middle of a linked list
  • Determining if a linked list is a palindrome
  • Finding the start of a cycle

Time Complexity: O(n) | Space Complexity: O(1)

How it works: The slow pointer moves one step at a time while the fast pointer moves two steps. If there's a cycle, they will eventually meet; if not, the fast pointer reaches the end.
