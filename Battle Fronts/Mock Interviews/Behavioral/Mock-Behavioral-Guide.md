## 📋 Mock Interview Structure (50 min)

| Phase | Duration | Content |
|-------|----------|---------|
| **Intro** | 3 min | Self-introduction / "Tell me about yourself" |
| **STAR Question #1** | 12 min | Full STARR answer + 2-3 probing follow-ups |
| **STAR Question #2** | 12 min | Different story, different dimension |
| **STAR Question #3** | 10 min | Shorter answer, tests quick recall |
| **"Why this company?" / Culture Fit** | 5 min | Demonstrate research and genuine interest |
| **"Questions for us?"** | 5 min | Ask 2-3 thoughtful questions |
| **Curveball** | 3 min | "If you could change one thing about your career..." |

> **Note:** For Format B (People Battle) mocks during Weeks 5/7/9/11, this is followed by a 50-min Coding Under Pressure round and a 20-min debrief. See [Mock-Interview-Protocol.md](../Mock-Interview-Protocol.md) for full Format B details.

## 🎤 Self-Introduction — "Tell Me About Yourself" (< 2 min)

Follow this 4-component structure (keep under 2 minutes, ideally ~60 seconds):

| Component | What to Say | Example |
|-----------|------------|---------|
| **1. Background** | Current role + years of experience | "I'm a backend developer with 2-3 years of experience, currently working with Django/DRF..." |
| **2. Highlight (KISS)** | 1-2 impressive achievements with metrics | "I built an automation bot that saved 3,000+ hours of manual work..." |
| **3. Why This Role** | Connect your experience to what they need | "I'm excited about this role because [specific reason tied to job description]..." |
| **4. Practiced Delivery** | Smooth, natural, confident — not robotic | Practice until it flows like a conversation, not a script |

**KISS Principle:** Keep It Short and Simple. Lead with your most impressive metric.

## 📖 STARR Framework — The Answer Structure

Upgrade from STAR to **STARR** for seniority signaling:

| Component | % of Answer | What to Include |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|
| **S** — Situation | 10% | Brief context. Set the scene in 2-3 sentences. |
| **T** — Task | 10% | Your specific responsibility. What was YOUR role? |
| **A** — Action | 60% | What YOU did (not the team). Be specific and technical. |
| **R** — Result | 15% | Quantifiable outcome. Metrics, impact, numbers. |
| **R** — Reflection | 5% | What you learned. How it changed your approach going forward. |

> **Why Reflection matters:** It signals seniority and growth mindset. Junior devs tell what happened. Mid/Senior devs tell what they LEARNED. Adding 1-2 sentences of reflection elevates every STARR answer.

**Example Reflection additions:**
- "That experience taught me to always validate assumptions with data before committing to an architecture."
- "Since then, I've applied that same debugging methodology to every production incident, reducing our mean time to resolution."

## 🎯 Question Selection

For each mock, pick questions that test different dimensions. Map each to one of Wiganz's 5 stories.

### Set A — Balanced Mock (covers all 8 dimensions)
1. "Tell me about yourself" → *Communication, Motivation*
2. "Tell me about your most impactful project" → *Proactivity, Company Values*
3. "Describe a time you dealt with an ambiguous situation" → *Unstructured Env*
4. "Tell me about a conflict with a colleague" → *Conflict Resolution, Empathy*
5. "Tell me about a time you failed" → *Growth, Perseverance*
6. "Why do you want to work here?" → *Motivation, Company Values*

### Set B — Technical-Heavy Mock
1. "What's the most challenging technical problem you solved?"
2. "Tell me about a project that didn't go as planned"
3. "How do you stay current with technology?"
4. "Describe a time you had to learn something quickly"
5. "What's the most interesting project you've worked on?"

### Set C — Leadership-Heavy Mock
1. "Give me an example of when you showed leadership"
2. "How do you mentor or help junior developers?"
3. "Tell me about a time you pushed for something"
4. "How do you handle disagreements with your manager?"
5. "What would you do in your first 90 days?"

### Set D — Stress Test / Curveball Mock
1. "Tell me about a time you had to push back on a stakeholder"
2. "Describe when you had to make a decision with incomplete information"
3. "What's one thing you'd change about your engineering career so far?"
4. "Tell me about the hardest feedback you've received"
5. "If you joined us and found the codebase was a mess, what would you do?"

## 🎬 Interviewer Scripts

### Opening
> "Hi! Thanks for chatting with me today. I'd love to learn more about you and your experience. Let's start — can you tell me a bit about yourself?"

### Follow-Up Probes (use 2-3 after each STARR answer)
> "You mentioned [X]. Can you tell me more about YOUR specific role in that?"
> "What was the hardest part of that experience?"
> "If you could do it again, what would you change?"
> "How did that experience change how you approach [related topic] now?"
> "What did other people on the team think about this?"
> "How did you measure the impact?"

### If Answer is Too Vague
> "Can you give me a specific example? Walk me through exactly what happened."

### If Answer Uses Too Much "We"
> "I appreciate the team effort — but what was YOUR specific contribution?"

### Time Warning (1 min left per question)
> "Great context. Can you wrap up with the result and what you took away from it?"

## 📊 Scoring System — Two Layers

### Layer 1: The 8-Dimension Rubric (TIH/Meta Framework)

This is the **overall assessment** of the candidate across the full interview. Each dimension is scored 1-4.

| Score | Label | Meaning |
|-------|-------|---------|
| 4 | **Strong Hire** | Exceeds expectations — clear, compelling, unprompted |
| 3 | **Leaning Hire** | Solid signals, minor gaps |
| 2 | **Leaning No Hire** | Partial signals, significant gaps |
| 1 | **Strong No Hire** | Cannot demonstrate this dimension |

#### 8 Dimensions — Detailed Rubric

| # | Dimension | What It Evaluates | 4 — Strong Hire | 3 — Leaning Hire | 2 — Leaning No Hire | 1 — Strong No Hire |
|---|-----------|-------------------|-----------------|------------------|---------------------|-------------------|
| 1 | **Motivation** | What drives you? Passion for impact, genuine enthusiasm for the role. | Compelling career narrative. Genuine passion for technologies and products with real impact. Clear direction. | Good reasons. Adequate enthusiasm. Understands the role. | Generic answers. No clear direction. "Just looking for a change." | Cannot articulate why this role or company. No visible drive. |
| 2 | **Proactivity** | Taking initiative and executing without being told. | Multiple self-started initiatives with measurable impact. Identifies problems AND solves them. | Shows initiative in some areas. Takes action when sees opportunity. | Mostly reactive. Waits for direction. Does what's assigned. | No evidence of self-starting behavior. Needs constant guidance. |
| 3 | **Unstructured Env** | Thriving in ambiguity — creating structure from chaos. | Takes full ownership in ambiguous situations. Creates frameworks, defines scope, drives clarity for the team. | Handles ambiguity with some support. Can navigate unclear requirements. | Struggles without clear direction. Needs well-defined tasks. | Paralyzed by ambiguity. Cannot function without explicit instructions. |
| 4 | **Perseverance** | Pushing through blockers and difficult problems. | Pushes through major obstacles. Finds alternative paths. Multiple examples of persistence leading to results. | Persists on most challenges. Doesn't give up easily. | Gives up when things get hard. Escalates too quickly. | Abandons at first difficulty. No evidence of grit. |
| 5 | **Conflict Resolution** | Handling disagreements and difficult relationships professionally. | Resolves conflicts professionally. Finds win-win solutions. Uses conflicts as growth opportunities. Handles CEO/VP-level pushback. | Handles conflict adequately. Can disagree respectfully. | Avoids conflict entirely or handles poorly (passive-aggressive, blame). | Cannot manage disagreements. Toxic patterns evident. |
| 6 | **Empathy** | Understanding others' perspectives, motivations, and feelings. | Deeply considers others' viewpoints. Adjusts approach based on others' needs. Builds strong relationships across roles. | Shows understanding of others' perspectives when prompted. | Limited awareness of impact on others. Self-focused narratives. | Cannot see things from others' perspective. Dismissive of others' concerns. |
| 7 | **Growth** | Self-awareness about strengths/weaknesses and commitment to improvement. | Seeks feedback actively. Learns from failure openly. Demonstrates continuous improvement with concrete examples. | Open to learning. Acknowledges mistakes when asked. | Defensive about mistakes. Slow to adapt. Weak self-awareness. | Blames others. No learning evident. No growth trajectory. |
| 8 | **Communication** | Clear, structured, compelling storytelling during the interview. | Structured (STARR), concise, compelling. Natural delivery. Adapts detail level to the question. | Clear answers. Mostly structured. Gets the point across. | Rambling, unfocused. Hard to follow. No clear structure. | Cannot tell a coherent story. Incoherent or contradictory. |

#### Seniority Calibration — Scope Is Everything

The **scope** of your stories determines the seniority level the interviewer assigns:

| Level | Expected Scope | Example |
|-------|---------------|---------|
| **Junior (IC4)** | Impact on YOUR work / your team's focus area. Individual effort. | "I refactored the auth module, reducing load time by 40%." |
| **Senior (IC5)** | Impact on ENTIRE TEAM (3+ people involved). Team-wide influence. | "I led the migration that involved 5 engineers and reduced deploy time by 60% across the team." |
| **Staff (IC6)** | Impact across ORGANIZATION / multiple teams (2+ teams). | "I drove the API standardization initiative across 3 teams, reducing integration bugs by 75%." |

> **Wiganz's target: Senior (IC5).** All 5 stories are calibrated to Senior scope. When telling stories, emphasize team-wide impact and multi-person coordination.

#### Hire Threshold

- **Hire:** Positive signals on **nearly all 8 dimensions** at target seniority level
- **No Hire:** Weak signals on 2+ dimensions, or stories consistently below target seniority scope
- **Phone Screen:** Only 1 interviewer — if they don't give "Leaning Hire" or above, you don't proceed to full loop

### Layer 2: Per-Answer Scoring (7 criteria × 1-4 scale)

Score **each individual STARR answer** on these 7 criteria to identify specific improvement areas:

| Criteria | 1 — Strong No Hire | 2 — Leaning No Hire | 3 — Leaning Hire | 4 — Strong Hire |
|----------|-------------------|---------------------|------------------|-----------------|
| **Structure** | No STARR visible. Stream of consciousness. | Attempts STARR but unclear flow, missing components. | Has STARR, generally clear. Minor flow issues. | Clean STARR, natural flow. Each component distinct and proportioned. |
| **Specificity** | Completely vague. "We did stuff." | Some details but mostly generic. No names/dates/numbers. | Good details. Some specific examples and context. | Rich details — names, dates, numbers, technical specifics. |
| **Action Focus** | All "we" or passive voice. YOUR role invisible. | Mix of "I" and "we" but YOUR contribution unclear. | Strong "I" focus. Clear what YOU specifically did. | Dominant "I" throughout Action. YOUR decisions, YOUR code, YOUR influence clearly separated from team. |
| **Impact / Results** | No metrics. "It went well." | Some qualitative impact. No numbers. | Quantified results. Clear before/after. | Multiple metrics, clear ROI. Business impact + technical impact. |
| **Reflection** | No reflection at all. | Brief generic takeaway. "I learned a lot." | Specific principle learned + how it applies going forward. | Deep reflection. Connects to broader engineering philosophy. Shows genuine growth. |
| **Seniority Signal** | Individual task scope only. | Individual scope with some team awareness. | Team scope (3+ people). Shows coordination/influence. | Team/org scope + mentorship + systemic change. |
| **Delivery** | Nervous, excessive filler words, can't maintain train of thought. | Steady but hesitant. Some filler ("um", "like"). | Confident, clear. Minor imperfections. Natural pace. | Engaging, natural, confident. Like telling a story to a friend. No filler words. |

**Per-Answer Score Thresholds:**
- **25-28/28:** Interview-ready answer
- **21-24/28:** Strong, needs minor polish
- **15-20/28:** Good foundation, practice more
- **Below 15:** Needs significant refinement

## 🗂️ Questions to Ask the Interviewer — "Do You Have Questions for Us?"

Always prepare 3-5 questions. Pick from categories relevant to the role/company:

### 🔧 Technical / Engineering
- What does the tech stack look like? Primary languages and frameworks?
- How do you handle technical debt? Is there dedicated time for refactoring?
- What does the code review process look like?
- How do you approach testing? What's your test coverage philosophy?
- What's the deployment process like? How often do you deploy?

### 👤 Role-Specific
- What does a typical day or week look like for this role?
- What would success look like in the first 90 days?
- What's the onboarding process for new engineers?
- What are the biggest challenges the team is facing right now?
- How is performance evaluated? What does the growth path look like?

### 🏢 Culture & Team
- How would you describe the team culture?
- How does the team handle disagreements on technical decisions?
- What's the work-life balance like on this team?
- How does remote collaboration work? What tools do you use?
- What do you personally enjoy most about working here?

### 🧭 Leadership & Company Direction
- What's the company's biggest priority for the next year?
- How does engineering influence product decisions?
- What's the company's approach to AI/emerging technologies?

### ⚠️ Questions to AVOID
- Anything easily found on the company website
- Salary/benefits in the first interview (unless they bring it up)
- "Do you have any concerns about my candidacy?" — too aggressive for most cultures

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes to Watch For

### Answer-Level Anti-Patterns
1. **The Rambler** — S+T takes over 1 minute (should be ~30 sec, 20% of answer)
2. **The "We" Trap** — Using "we" in the Action section instead of "I"
3. **The Skipped Reflection** — Forgetting to add the R(eflection) at the end
4. **The Memorizer** — Sounds rehearsed and robotic, not natural
5. **The Avoider** — Gives a different story when pressed with follow-ups
6. **The Humble Brag** — "My biggest weakness is I work too hard"
7. **No Numbers** — Results without quantification ("it was better")

### Interview-Level Anti-Patterns
8. **One-Story Wonder** — Using the same story for every question
9. **Junior Scope** — All stories are individual-only, no team impact
10. **No Questions Back** — Saying "No, I think you covered everything" at the end
11. **Company Ignorance** — Cannot answer "Why this company?" with specifics
12. **Negativity Leak** — Badmouthing current/previous employer or colleagues
13. **Clock Ignorance** — Not managing time across questions (spending 8 min on Q1, rushing Q3)

## 🚀 How to Run a Mock with Hadriel

Say: **"Hadriel, let's do a mock behavioral interview"**

Hadriel will:
1. Act as the interviewer (stays in character the whole time)
2. Open with "Tell me about yourself"
3. Ask 3-5 behavioral questions with follow-up probes
4. Press for specifics when answers are vague
5. Keep time and warn when an answer runs long
6. Stay in interviewer mode until the mock ends
7. Switch to trainer mode for the debrief
8. Score using both Layer 1 (8-dimension) and Layer 2 (per-answer) rubrics
9. Give detailed, actionable feedback
10. Track progress over time

### Optional Modes
- **"Mock behavioral — company: [name]"** → Hadriel tailors questions to that company's values
- **"Mock behavioral — stress test"** → Aggressive follow-ups, curveballs, rapid-fire
- **"Mock behavioral — dimension drill: [dimension]"** → Focuses questions on weakest area
- **"Mock behavioral — speed round"** → 6 questions in 20 minutes, trains quick recall
